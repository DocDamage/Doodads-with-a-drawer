# Changelog

## Unreleased — 2.0.0-alpha.1

- A Memao creator beside the LPC one (More > Characters): reads the Memao Sprite Sheet Creator's asset pack from a folder on this computer (it never goes into the project or the plugin), and builds characters in the same Character Creator window, with its own colour ramps, sleeves that follow the arms, a Tools tab, and all 22 animations. Export writes 48-pixel MZ sheets: classic and smooth walkers with idle and run, actions, a side-view battler and a face. The creator window now serves both libraries through a kit (`DocsDoodads.CreatorKits`).
- The Character Creator's "Only art safe to encrypt" (on for a new project) says how many parts it lets through, and where it hides parts in the tab in view, a line says how many, with **Show all parts** to turn it off.
- The Character Creator offers the big weapons that only have the library's bigger art: long spear, dragon spear, trident, katana, scimitar, the other longsword, club and boomerang. Before, they couldn't be picked.
- The Character Creator draws big weapons (the LPC library's oversize and 128-pixel art: most swords, spears and polearms) at their size instead of leaving them out. The stage shows the whole swing. Action sheets keep the whole bigger frame (`$Name_slash[f6]` at 192 × 192, the tip of a downward thrust included) and stand on the feet; walkers and the side-view battler get bigger frames that keep what is above the feet (a battler with 192 × 128 cells). The export says which. Visual Equipment parts get the same frames, and battler layers with bigger cells are drawn at their own size.
- A Backswing action (`$Name_slash_reverse[f6]`): the slash played backwards, with the return-swing art of the weapons that have it. The club and boomerang swing that way on Slash too.
- Battle animations aim at the figure on a side-view battler whose cells are bigger than it (the Character Creator's battlers grown for a big weapon, or `[hN]` in a battler's name), instead of showing high.
- `[bN]` in a character sheet's name: its frames have N pixels of art below the feet, and the character still stands where it is, with its shadow, reflection, bush and gear following the feet.
- The LPC compositor ignores empty padding columns on 64-pixel sheets too (the wand's slash was left out).
- A Playful theme (More > Look): cream cards, teal and coral, yellow for what is picked, rounder corners and pill buttons. Filled buttons keep white text at 4.5:1 or better.
- The Character Creator's outfit as Visual Equipment parts: the character's sheets become the body only, each piece it wears gets part sheets in the same layouts (walker, idle, run, actions, battler; front and behind pieces in one part slot) with its notetag, which can be copied or put on a weapon, armour or actor (backed up, and live in the playtest). Equipping and unequipping then changes what the character wears.
- Visual Equipment layers follow the body's idle, run and action sheets: `Hat` draws `Hat_idle[f2]` while the body shows `$Hero_idle[f2]`, when the file is there.
- The Character Creator also exports a side-view battler (MZ's 18 motions) and action sheets for Play Character Action (slash, thrust, spellcast, shoot, hurt, and more on request).
- Export to RPG Maker from the Character Creator: a classic `$Name` walker, a smooth `$Name[f9][w2]` walker with `_idle` and `_run` sheets and their animation set, and a face in `img/faces` (other faces kept), with credits recorded and CREDITS_LPC.txt rewritten; Try on the player.
- `[wN]` in a sheet name (with `[fN]`): frame 0 stands and the others walk, one cycle every N tiles at any speed.
- Run sheets no longer flash back to the walk for a frame between tiles.
- The Characters card's "Only art that is safe to encrypt" is saved with the project (it could be lost before).
- Character Creator (More > Characters > Open the Creator): a bright, rounded window to make a character from the LPC library, with tabs of pickers and colour swatches, a stage that turns and plays every animation, dice, saved characters, credits, and keyboard and gamepad control; one side at a time in a small window.
- LPC compositor: stacks the library's parts in their order for each body type, with their colours (skin matched across the body, head and ears), fixed-colour variants, faces that follow the head, and parts that need others; recoloured layers are cached. Characters > Try it shows a random character walking.
- Characters (More): the LPC character library on this computer. Scan its parts, colours and credits into the editor's cache; the project setting "Only art that is safe to encrypt (CC0, OGA-BY)"; credits recorded per picture and written to CREDITS_LPC.txt; and Problems for pictures that can't be encrypted and credits that need writing out. A library inside the project is warned about, since Deployment would copy it into the game.
- LPC character creator, phase 0: a plan (docs/LPC_CHARACTER_CREATOR_PLAN.md) and a throwaway spike, `node scripts/lpc_spike.js <library> <project> [Name]`, that indexes a local copy of the Universal LPC generator's library, recolours and stacks the parts of one character, and writes a `$Name.png` walker, a `$Name[f9].png` walker and the credits of the files used. No LPC code, data or art ships with the plugin.
- 3D models: morph targets (shape keys) and their animations, normal maps, light per pixel (sharp highlights on flat faces), a selection box that follows the pose, and, in World > 3D Models, drawing on the graphics card (skins posed, placed and projected in the shader, each model drawn with a depth buffer), falling back to the CPU on round worlds, big skins or a card that refuses the shader. The shaders compile on WebGL 1 and 2.
- Tiles (top bar): paint MZ's own tiles in a playtest from the map's tileset (A1 to E, blocks of B to E tiles), with autotiles joining up as in MZ's editor (shapes checked against MZ's tables), automatic or chosen layers, an eraser, Alt+click to pick up, one undo step a stroke, saved into the map file.
- Shaped lights: cones that point a way, light presets (torch, candle, campfire, lantern, neon, crystal, spotlight, moonbeam, window glow), walls that stop light on flat maps (impassable tiles and listed regions cast shadows), and a flashlight the player carries that turns to face, with a Flashlight plugin command.
- Docking: drag the Item Bag or Details by its title to the left, right or bottom (or choose in More > Look); edges facing the map resize them, two panels can share a side or the bottom, and where they are is remembered per layout (Save Layout keeps it too). The status bar, hint and readouts follow the map's area.
- Accessibility: names for every control and panel for screen readers, messages read out, the Item Bag as a list the arrow keys move through (Enter picks), F6 and Shift+F6 between panels, a visible focus ring, and Less motion (More > Look, or the system's reduced-motion setting).
- A language layer for the editor: translations in data/DocsDoodadsLanguages/<code>.json, keyed by the English words (with {name} for values), picked in More > Look; untranslated words stay English. `npm run strings` writes a template of every English string. Only English ships.
- Background and overlay layers on flat maps (the map's settings): pictures behind the tiles or over the map, each following the camera by its own amount, scrolling, zooming, repeating and blending, plus soft fog banks drawn by the game that drift with the wind.
- Weather on flat maps (World): wind that leans swaying doodads, pushes particles and fog and blows leaves across the screen (stronger in rain and storms, with gusts; Set Wind changes it), and lightning in storms with thunder after the flash, sooner for near strikes.
- The camera on flat maps can ease after the player and look ahead, stay inside a region's area while the player is in it (a room), follow an event (2D Camera Follow), and glide along a path for cutscenes (2D Camera Path), then come back or stay. With none of these, MZ's own scrolling is unchanged.
- The corner minimap works on flat maps too (World > Minimap, or each map's own choice), with an optional fog of war: the minimap and the world map show only what the player has been near, kept per map in the save. Show / Hide Minimap and Explored Map plugin commands; a size setting.
- Room stamps (More > Rooms): drag over the map to save its tiles on every layer, the doodads standing in it and its events as a room, and place it on any map with a click, as one undo step. Empty upper layers keep the map's tiles; parts off the map are left off; events get new ids.
- Doodad packs (More > Packs): prefabs (with the prefabs inside), rooms, biomes, dressing rules and collections, with the files they use and their Item Bag entries, as one .ddpack file to bring into another project. Bringing one in shows what differs, keeps your files unless told otherwise (backing them up), renames clashing ids and never writes outside the project's art and sound folders.
- Keyboard shortcuts come from one table: More > Keys (or Keyboard Shortcuts in the palette, or ?) lists every key by group and changes any of them: click a key, press the new keys, Esc keeps the old ones. A key already in use asks before it moves; the editor key, Esc and the arrows can't be taken. Tools and views (Stamp, Hills, Show Collision...) can be given keys too. Tooltips, menus and the palette show your keys; they are kept on this computer. Letters follow the keyboard layout (Ctrl+Z is the key marked Z on AZERTY). Shortcuts now need their exact modifiers (Ctrl+G no longer toggles the grid). The guide's shortcut table is written from the same table (`npm run shortcuts`), and its old names are gone.
- Prefab copies keep their own settings: change a setting in Details on one copy and it is marked ● as that copy's own; Sync Copies keeps it while the rest follows the master, and Use Prefab's gives it back. Copies keep their id, lock and editor-hidden flag through a sync. A selection holding a whole copy of another prefab saves it inside the new prefab: syncing the inner prefab changes the copies of every prefab that holds it, and deleting it leaves its doodads in them.
- Screen Looks by time and by region: game-wide looks that ease in and out between two times of day (a warm dusk, a blue night), and looks that fade in while the player stands on a region (game-wide, or a map's own, which win for the same region: a cold crypt), each a preset or a copy of the look being edited, with a strength. A map can leave the time looks out (interiors). Set Look back to the map brings them back; the clock bar previews times and the eye button previews a region.
- Undo keeps each map's steps: going to another map (Go to Map, map tabs, Go To in Problems) parks this map's steps, and coming back brings them back (a toast says how many), unless the map was changed in MZ meanwhile. The last ten maps keep theirs; Steps shows this map's and says how many other maps have steps waiting.
- Regions tool (top bar): paint MZ region numbers onto tiles (0 rubs out; Alt+click picks one up; [ and ] change the size), one undo step a stroke, saved with the map. The map shows each region's colour and number, and its card lists the regions on the map, how many tiles each covers and what the project uses it for (climbing, ice, no flying, water reflections, footsteps, ambience, background sounds...). Terrain tags (set in MZ's Tilesets) can be shown on the map too.
- `DocsDoodads.api` for other plugins and scripts: find doodads (by name, picture, id or nearby tiles), place, move, change and remove them for this run of the game, set their states and lights (kept in the save), read and set the clock, season and ambient light, and listen for touches and state and light changes.
- Traversal on gamepads and touch screens: jump and fly go on gamepad buttons MZ leaves free (the triggers by default), and touch screens get round on-screen Jump (or Dive) and Fly (or Land) buttons in the corner; World > Traversal picks the buttons, when the on-screen ones show, and their size.
- Doodad data saves with its keys in a fixed order, so saving the same map gives the same file. The plugin parameter **Doodad Files: One file per map** keeps each map's doodads in its own file under `data/DocsDoodads/` for smaller diffs and fewer merge conflicts in Git; maps that lose all their doodads lose their file (after a backup).
- Doodad states (Details > States): named looks for one doodad (lit and unlit, open and closed, whole and broken), each with its own art, and with or without its light and collision. A switch, a variable or events pick the state; the old picture crossfades into the new. Preview shows each state while editing.
- World state in saves: the plugin commands Set Doodad State, Set Doodad Light and Store Doodad State, and `DocsDoodads.WorldState` for scripts (`is('Front Door', 'Open')`), find doodads by the Name given in Details and keep what they set with the save file. A saved state wins over the switch or variable until it is cleared.
- Touchable doodads (Details > Touch): a common event runs when the player presses Action facing the doodad (or clicks it), or walks into it, and it can step to its next state on its own (a door that opens and closes). Problems lists state art and common events that are missing.
- Problems (More, or the palette) lists what is probably a mistake, on this map or every map: pictures and sounds that aren't in the project, doodads standing outside the map, lamps on maps that never get dark, events nobody can step on, and events under a doodad's collision. Each has Go To (it centres the map and picks the doodad or event, going to another map first if needed) and, where safe, a Fix (Replace Art, Remove Sound, Move Inside), each one undo step.
- Check Reach floods the tiles the player can walk to from where they stand (or from a clicked tile), through walls, doodad collision and characters in the way, shades them on the map, and lists the doors, chests and transfers out of reach, noting when only a character is in the way.
- Doodads drawn as a strip of frames have an Animation section in Details: frames across and down (the picture is split evenly), speed, a start frame, and how to play: loop, back and forth, once when a switch turns on (and again each time it does), or hold one frame.
- Capture (More, or the palette) saves a picture of the map as it is on screen, without the editor's outlines, or a looping GIF of the 3D camera turning once round the view (12, 24 or 36 frames, 320 to 640 pixels wide; with a progress pill and Cancel), into the project's `screenshots` folder.
- The right-click menu learns which actions are used most and, in longer menus, offers up to eight of them as a row of buttons at the top.
- The first time each tool is picked (Stamp, Scatter, Paint, Path, Hills, Grab, Listen), a short tour of two or three steps points at its options and the map; "No more tours" turns them off, and How to Use replays them.
- How to Use can add sample items to try: four small pictures drawn into `img/doodads/samples` (bush, flowers, rock, lamp post), the Sample Garden and Sample Lamp Post prefabs, and a Sample Meadow biome (added once; pictures already there are kept).
- After an update, a What's New card lists what changed, once (also in How to Use and the palette).
- With several doodads picked, Details shows "—" where their values differ (a half-ticked box, a "mixed" choice) and one edit sets them all; a Placement section sets their X, Y, Z-order, turn and size. Number boxes in Details change by dragging their label left and right and step with the arrow keys (Shift for ten steps).
- A picked doodad has handles: drag a corner to resize it around its foot (Shift keeps its proportions) or the knob above it to turn it (Shift snaps to 15 degrees), with a readout beside the cursor; each drag is one undo step.
- The command palette (Ctrl+K) matches words in any order, lists recently used commands first (marked Recent), shows shortcuts as key caps, and takes values typed straight in: "time 18:30" or "6pm", "go to map 3", "season winter", "size 125".
- Map tabs under the top bar list the maps opened recently (up to 8): a click goes there, × takes one out, and a dot marks maps with unsaved changes.
- The Item Bag has a folder view (the folder button beside the item count): the items in a tree of their folders, opened folders remembered, with the search and tabs still applying.
- A clock at the top of the map panel previews any time of day and season while editing (lamps, shadows, the 3D sky and season art follow it); ▶ runs the day fast and Now goes back to the game's own time.
- Show Sounds (More) draws how far each sound source reaches and shades tiles with a region sound. The Listen tool (More, or the palette) plays what the player would hear standing on a clicked tile, sound sources, region sound and ambience together at their volumes, lists them, and draws how far each ambience layer listens.
- Show Speed (More) shows frames a second, draw calls a frame, the pictures loaded, doodads and lights on the map, and says when a map goes over a typical budget (with what to try).
- More > Look sets the editor's size (100, 125 or 150%), a light, dark or high-contrast theme, and the map colours: standard, or colour-blind safe (the Okabe-Ito colours) for selection, guides, the mirror line, paths, Old Saves, the Scatter and Hills brushes, collision and blocked tiles, with a key to what each colour means. Kept on this computer; also in the palette. The theme's surface colours are now all theme tokens.
- Details remembers which sections were open, and a ☆ on each section pins it to the top (in the order pinned).
- Long jobs show a progress pill with a bar and Cancel at the top right of the map, without blocking the editor: finding art when the editor opens (only when it takes a moment), packing map art (Cancel writes nothing), Auto-Slice on big sheets, and bringing in files (the import card now closes and the import carries on in its pill).
- Every message is kept: More > Messages lists them newest first, to read one again. Deleting doodads, an event or a layer, erasing with Scatter, clearing map dressing and Replace Art show an Undo button on their message, which stays while the mouse is on it.
- Editor buttons show a themed tooltip after a short rest, with the shortcut in a key cap, instead of the browser's plain one; buttons with only an icon keep their name for screen readers.
- The Item Bag sorts by best match, name, newest first, biggest first, most placed or last placed (remembered; Recent and Look-alikes keep their own order). New files in the library remember when they were added.
- Search terms show as chips under the search box, each with × to take it out, and a search can be saved under a name as a chip of its own (kept with the library, like collections; also in the palette).
- Resting the mouse on an item shows a card with its size, frame count, default depth, how often it was placed and where it is used, with Details, Cut Up and Where Used buttons.
- An empty Item Bag tab says what goes there and offers a button: bring in files, show everything, or clear the search.
- Show Collision (More, or the palette) draws every doodad's collision box, coloured by preset, shades the tiles doodads block in red and the tiles the map blocks in grey, with a key to the colours; doodads hidden in game are drawn faintly and block nothing. Flat maps only; the choice is remembered.
- While dragging, a readout beside the cursor gives the doodad's position, tile and size in pixels and tiles. Smart guides also line up base lines (where doodads stand) and, when nothing else is near, snap edges and base lines to tile lines (teal).
- A status bar along the bottom of the map shows the tile and pixel under the cursor, the map's number and name, what is picked, the active layer, the zoom, and whether everything is saved (and when it last was).
- Scatter has an eraser: right-drag takes away the picked item's doodads inside the brush circle (not locked ones, nor ones on hidden or locked layers), one undo step a stroke. Scatter now shows its circle on the map, green to paint and red to erase.
- The Hills brush has a hard or soft edge (a hard edge changes the whole circle the same), shows each tile's height as a number on the tiles under it, and `[` and `]` change the size of Scatter, Paint and Hills.
- The Editor Toggle Key can be any key or a chord, such as `Ctrl+F10` or `Ctrl+Shift+E`, for projects where another plugin owns F10 (a key that types, such as a letter, is left alone while typing in a field). The Done button's tip shows the key.
- More ways to select: Select All (Ctrl+A), Select Same Art, Select Everything on a layer and Invert Selection, in the map's right-click menu and the command palette. Right-clicking one of several picked doodads keeps them all picked, so the menu acts on all of them.
- Paste in Place (Ctrl+Shift+V, or the right-click menu) puts copied doodads back at the position they were copied from, on this map or another.
- Replace Art: give the picked doodads, or every doodad showing a picture on this map or on every map, a different picture while their position, size, depth, motion, lights and collision stay (one undo step; locked doodads stay as they are). From a doodad's right-click menu, the palette, or an Item Bag picture's right-click menu (Replace Art on Maps).
- The editor asks its questions in its own cards instead of the browser's prompt, confirm and alert boxes (slicer grid, layout, collection, template, prefab, effect and file names, recovery, replacing sheets, restoring a backup, sounds dropped on the map). Cards match the look, fit a small window, answer with Enter and cancel with Esc; placing an asset as an event picks its template from a list. Deleting a layer that has doodads, a collection, a layout, a prefab or restoring a backup gets a plain sentence and a red button, and the save-conflict card is one of them.
- Unsaved work is no longer lost by accident: Done, the toggle key, closing the Playtest window and reloading it (F5) ask Save, Don't Save or Keep Editing when something isn't saved. Don't Save puts the doodads and events back as they were last saved. The Save button shows an orange dot while there are unsaved changes and says "Saved" for a moment after a save.

Checked against MZ's own build with the same key: every file is identical except `System.json`, which MZ saves again from the editor.

The Item Bag (left) and Details (right) fold away to small tabs; a hint bubble says what to do next, a welcome card greets first-time users, toasts are pills and buttons can play the project's System sounds. - Faster 3D terrain: on a 200 × 200 world map with view distance 64, terrain and a storm went from 18 to 58 fps. The terrain no longer allocates per frame, sorts with a radix sort, is rebuilt every few tiles instead of every tile, and draws level ground and fully faded ground more cheaply; the 3D and terrain settings are worked out once a frame.
- The README and guide now say RPG Maker MZ only: nothing since 2.0 has been tested in MV.

- Added 3D quality levels: the players' Effects Quality option (High, Medium, Low, Off) now also scales the 3D view down for slower machines, with a shorter view distance (fog pulled in to match), fewer weather drops, no wakes on Low and simpler hills. The option now shows in Options whenever a map uses the 3D view. The editor always shows maps as set.

- Added 3D-only height settings for standing doodads and characters, so tall tileset art can be brought in proportion in the 3D view without changing the 2D map; editor picking and boxes match.
- Added day and night to the 3D view: a sky that follows the clock (day blue, warm sunrise and sunset, night with twinkling stars), a sun and moon crossing the sky on the shadows' path, hill shading and water glints from the current sun or moon, and fog that fades into the sky.
- Added 3D weather: MZ's Set Weather Effect (rain, storm, snow) falls through the 3D world around the camera, with wind-slanted rain that splashes on the ground and raised terrain, raindrop ripples on water, drifting snow, fog fading, night dimming and MZ's weather darkening. Per map: on/off and density.
- Added rippling water to the 3D view: moving waves bend what shows through water tiles and catch the light (dimmer at night), shores stay dry, and boats, ships and anything moving through water leave spreading wake rings with foam. Works on flat ground, round worlds and raised terrain, and across the seam of looping maps. Strength, speed, sparkle and wakes are set per map.
- Added terrain height to the 3D view: mountains and forests rise automatically on world tilesets, with heights by terrain tag, by region, or painted with a Terrain brush (raise, lower, flatten, set, erase; one undo step per stroke). Smooth (rounded hills and ranges) or stepped (cliffs) ground, slope shading, hills hiding what's behind them, characters standing on the ground, doodads kept to level ground (red boxes mark any left on a mountain), clicks on slopes, optional blocking of steep steps, and round worlds supported.
- Added curvature control for round worlds: a Curvature slider in the 3D View panel (0 flat to 100 tiny planet) and curvature and shape on Set Camera, eased with the camera, so a cutscene can turn a flat map into a planet and back. Ray tests now stay accurate for nearly flat curves.
- Added round worlds to the 3D view: a planet (curves away all round, with a radius that can fit a looping map so one lap is the map's width) or a rolling log (curves away ahead), with things past the horizon hidden, optional leaning with the curve, and clicks, fog and editor boxes on the curve.
- Fixed 3D billboards: lighting tints were not applied, and scaled sprites showed thin lines from neighbouring atlas pixels along their edges.
- Added a 3D view for any map: tiles, floor doodads, shadows, reflections and lighting on a perspective ground plane; characters, gear and standing doodads as distance-sorted billboards; camera tilt, turn, field of view, zoom, radial fog and a parallax sky; optional Q/W camera turning with camera-relative arrow keys and facings; touch-to-move through the ground; editor picking, dragging and yellow selection boxes in 3D; and the Set Camera, Reset Camera and Set 3D View plugin commands.
- Added visual equipment: <visual> notetags on weapons, armors, states, actors and classes build characters from layered parts (paper doll) or draw gear in front of or behind them. Named parts act as slots, so armor replaces the body's shirt, and "up" sets a different order while facing up (capes, shields). Also base layers, hide tags, colours from switches and variables, {alias} image names for body types, and side-view sheets for battlers. Layers show on the player, followers, tagged events (or events wearing an actor's look), side-view battlers, water reflections and the save screen, and the equip screen previews the highlighted item. The map panel has a try-on preview and a notetag builder. Plugin commands: Set Visual Alias, Add/Remove Visual Layer, Set Visual Colour.
- Added an animated map destination marker: drawn shapes in any colour, images with frames, looping database animations or VFX, blend, size, pulse, spin and bob, above characters or on the ground, and optional dots along the path. The Set Destination Marker plugin command changes it mid-game.
- Added editor tools: per-map layers (show/hide in the editor, lock, hide in the game, active layer); symmetry (left/right, top/bottom, four ways) for stamps and brushes; a biome brush with weighted art, spacing, edge thinning and patchiness; dressing rules that place props along water edges, at wall bases, beside or inside regions, on terrain tags or open ground; a visual save history that outlines and restores single doodads and events; animated drawer thumbnails; and a Ctrl+K command palette.
- New doodads get a uid so history can follow them.
- Added spatial sound: event and move-route sound effects get quieter with distance and pan by direction, with automatic pitch and volume variation; ambient loops and repeating sound effects on doodads and events (<sound: ...>); per-map echo; background sounds by region with fades; <spatial>/<no spatial> tags; and the Play SE At plugin command.
- Added particle emitters on doodads (11 presets, adjustable amount, layer and offset), grass and bushes that bend when walked through and can drop leaves, and a VFX builder: effects made of particle and sprite-sheet layers, six built-ins, saved with the project, played with the Play VFX plugin command or looped on a doodad.
- Added character animation: sheet names set frames per row ([f8] or %(8)), single-row ([d1], mirrored) and 8-direction ([d8]) layouts; idle and run cycles from Name_idle / Name_run sheets with auto-detect and preloading; Play/Clear Character Action with hold, loops and wait; and optional diagonal movement for the player.
- Added post-processing: bloom, colour grading, vignette, film grain, chromatic aberration, lens distortion, CRT, pixelate, sharpen and tilt-shift, with nine presets, a live builder (Post FX), a game-wide look plus per-map looks, hold-to-compare, quality levels and an Effects Quality option for players. Plugin commands: Set Post-Processing Look (fades) and Play Screen Effect (impact zoom, shockwave, chromatic punch, bloom flash).

Plugin commands: Set Time of Day, Pass Time, Clock Control, Set Season, Set Atmosphere Effect. Event tags: <shadow>, <no shadow>, <motion blur>, <no motion blur>, <no reflection>.
